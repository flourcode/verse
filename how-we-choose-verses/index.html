How We Choose Verses

Scripture, not fortune cookies. That line is on our home page because it’s the whole method. A verse pulled out of its passage can be made to say almost anything. We try not to do that.

It’s also what “better” means in our name. A better verse is not a favorite or a highlight. It’s the one whose setting genuinely matches the moment you’re in, chosen by someone who read the surrounding chapter and can tell you why it fits.

The rules

  1. Read the paragraph, not just the line. Before a verse goes on a page, we read what comes before and after it. If the surrounding passage is about something else entirely, the verse doesn’t go on the page, even if the sentence sounds perfect on its own.
  2. The context has to connect to the topic. A verse for a job interview should come from a passage about facing something with your knees shaking, not from a passage about the end of the world that happens to contain the word “courage”.
  3. Say why it fits, specifically. Every verse on a topic page has a short note: who said it, to whom, and what was going on. If we can’t write that note honestly in a few sentences, the verse doesn’t belong on the page.
  4. Prefer the plain reading. We don’t stretch metaphors or lean on one unusual translation choice. If a verse only “fits” after a lot of explaining, it’s the wrong verse.
  5. Keep promises the text actually makes. Some verses were written to a specific person or nation about a specific event. We include them when the principle carries, and we say so in the note, rather than presenting them as personal guarantees.
  6. Balance the page. A good topic page has variety: a psalm, something from the Gospels, a letter, maybe a proverb. Different kinds of writing help in different ways. Lament sits next to reassurance on purpose.
  7. Fewer, better. Each page has seven to ten verses. We could list forty. Forty is a search result, not a recommendation.

What we leave out

  • Verses that are mostly famous for being on posters, where the poster meaning and the passage meaning don’t match.
  • Passages that exist mainly to settle doctrinal arguments. That isn’t what this site is for.
  • Any verse we would need to trim mid-sentence to make it fit. Where a range is shown (for example, Philippians 4:6–7), you get the whole range.

Where the text comes from

All verse text is taken directly from the World English Bible, a public-domain translation, and is loaded from the published text rather than retyped. References are shown with the verse so you can check any of them in the translation you normally read.

When we get it wrong

We will. If you think a verse is out of place, or a note misreads the passage, tell us. Corrections are made to the page directly; we don’t keep a version we know is wrong.
