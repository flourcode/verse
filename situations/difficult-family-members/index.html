Bible verses for difficult family members

You can leave a job, but family stays family. That's what makes a difficult relative uniquely draining. These verses are about your side of the relationship: your words, your temper, your willingness to forgive, and your limits.

If a family member is abusive, Scripture about peace and patience doesn't ask you to stay in harm's way. Safety first; the rest can be worked out from a distance.

A verse to start with

For difficult family members

Romans 12:17-18

Repay no one evil for evil. Respect what is honorable in the sight of all men. If it is possible, as much as it is up to you, be at peace with all men.

World English Bible (WEB)

Why this fits

'If it is possible, as much as it is up to you, be at peace with all men.' Paul admits peace isn't always possible and isn't entirely up to you. With family, that honesty matters. Your responsibility is your part: don't repay evil, act honorably. The other person's part isn't yours to carry.

7 verses for difficult family members

  1. Romans 12:17-18

    Repay no one evil for evil. Respect what is honorable in the sight of all men. If it is possible, as much as it is up to you, be at peace with all men.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Letter (epistle)

    'If it is possible, as much as it is up to you, be at peace with all men.' Paul admits peace isn't always possible and isn't entirely up to you. With family, that honesty matters. Your responsibility is your part: don't repay evil, act honorably. The other person's part isn't yours to carry.

  2. Proverbs 17:14

    The beginning of strife is like breaching a dam, therefore stop contention before quarreling breaks out.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Wisdom literature

    'Stop contention before quarreling breaks out.' The image is a dam breaching: once the water's moving, you can't stop it. Family arguments have a familiar early moment where things could still go either way. This proverb says leave then. The verse is about timing. It doesn't ask you to win the argument or avoid the person forever. It asks you to notice the moment before the moment, when a subject change or a walk to the kitchen still works.

  3. Proverbs 15:1

    A gentle answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Wisdom literature

    'A gentle answer turns away wrath.' With relatives, you often know exactly what will set them off. This proverb puts the lever in your hand: your reply can escalate or de-escalate, and you get to choose, even when they don't.

  4. Colossians 3:13

    bearing with one another, and forgiving each other, if any man has a complaint against any; even as Christ forgave you, so you also do.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Letter (epistle)

    'Bearing with one another, and forgiving each other, if any man has a complaint against any.' Bearing with is tolerating the ongoing friction; forgiving is releasing the specific wrong. Family requires both, repeatedly. The standard is Christ's forgiveness of you, which is meant to be humbling, not crushing.

  5. 1 Peter 3:9

    not rendering evil for evil, or insult for insult; but instead blessing, knowing that you were called to this, that you may inherit a blessing.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Letter (epistle)

    'Not rendering evil for evil, or insult for insult; but instead blessing.' Peter is realistic about insults. Family insults land harder because they know where to aim. The instruction isn't to absorb them silently but to respond with blessing, which is a choice, not a feeling.

  6. Proverbs 19:11

    The discretion of a man makes him slow to anger. It is his glory to overlook an offense.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Wisdom literature

    'It is his glory to overlook an offense.' Not every remark at a family dinner needs a response. Overlooking is a skill and, this proverb says, an honor. It's also the only way to survive some holidays. Discretion in the first half of the verse is what makes overlooking possible: knowing which remarks are worth answering. Most aren't. Proverbs treats restraint as a mark of maturity, not weakness.

  7. Ephesians 4:26

    “Be angry, and don’t sin.” Don’t let the sun go down on your wrath,

    World English Bible (WEB) · Letter (epistle)

    'Be angry, and don't sin.' The anger is allowed; it's what you do with it. The deadline, before the sun goes down, keeps a single argument from becoming a decades-long estrangement. Many family rifts began as one unresolved evening. Paul's next line is 'don't give place to the devil,' which is his way of saying that anger left overnight becomes a foothold. The verse doesn't require the argument to be resolved by bedtime, only that it not be abandoned there.

Common questions

What does the Bible say about toxic family members?

Romans 12:18 sets the frame: be at peace as far as it depends on you. Proverbs 17:14 and 19:11 are about avoiding and overlooking fights. Nothing in these passages requires you to remain in harm's way.

Is there a Bible verse about forgiving family?

Colossians 3:13 applies directly, and Matthew 18:21-22 (seventy times seven) was Peter's question about a brother.
