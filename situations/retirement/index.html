Bible verses for retirement

Retirement isn't in the Bible, but growing older and finishing well are, repeatedly. Scripture expects the later years to be fruitful, not idle. These verses are for someone stepping out of one role and wondering what the next one is.

A verse to start with

For retirement

Psalm 92:14

They will still produce fruit in old age. They will be full of sap and green,

World English Bible (WEB)

Why this fits

'They will still produce fruit in old age.' The psalm compares the righteous to trees planted in God's house that stay green. It's a direct answer to the fear that retirement means becoming useless. Fruit in old age is the expectation, not the exception.

7 verses for retirement

  1. Psalm 92:14

    They will still produce fruit in old age. They will be full of sap and green,

    World English Bible (WEB) · Psalm (poetry)

    'They will still produce fruit in old age.' The psalm compares the righteous to trees planted in God's house that stay green. It's a direct answer to the fear that retirement means becoming useless. Fruit in old age is the expectation, not the exception.

  2. Isaiah 46:4

    Even to old age I am he, and even to gray hairs I will carry you. I have made, and I will bear. Yes, I will carry, and will deliver.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Prophecy

    'Even to old age I am he, and even to gray hairs I will carry you.' God contrasts himself with idols that have to be carried around. He carries. For a season where you're no longer the one holding everything up at work, this is the right verse.

  3. 2 Timothy 4:7

    I have fought the good fight. I have finished the course. I have kept the faith.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Letter (epistle)

    'I have fought the good fight. I have finished the course. I have kept the faith.' Paul, near the end. Retirement is a course finished, and this is what finishing well sounds like. The verse is often read at funerals but fits any ending done with integrity.

  4. Psalm 71:18

    Yes, even when I am old and gray-haired, God, don’t forsake me, until I have declared your strength to the next generation, your might to everyone who is to come.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Psalm (poetry)

    An older person's prayer: don't forsake me until I've told the next generation what you've done. This verse gives retirement a job: pass it on. Whatever you learned over a career is meant to be declared to someone younger. The psalmist is honest about being 'old and gray-haired' and asks not to be abandoned. Then the request turns outward: let me declare your strength to the next generation. Retirement is often when people finally have time to do exactly that.

  5. Ecclesiastes 3:1

    For everything there is a season, and a time for every purpose under heaven:

    World English Bible (WEB) · Wisdom literature

    'For everything there is a season.' The famous list that follows includes planting and uprooting, keeping and throwing away. Retirement is a season change, and Ecclesiastes gives you permission to treat it as one rather than an ending. The list that follows pairs each thing with its opposite: build and break down, keep silent and speak. None of them is called wrong. A season of working hard and a season of not working are both, in this view, appropriate in their time.

  6. Philippians 1:6

    being confident of this very thing, that he who began a good work in you will complete it until the day of Jesus Christ.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Letter (epistle)

    'He who began a good work in you will complete it.' The work God is doing in a person doesn't stop when employment does. Paul is confident about completion, and the timeline extends to 'the day of Christ Jesus,' well past any retirement date.

  7. Psalm 90:12

    So teach us to count our days, that we may gain a heart of wisdom.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Psalm (poetry)

    'Teach us to count our days, that we may gain a heart of wisdom.' Retirement makes the counting real. The verse turns that into an asset: knowing time is limited produces wisdom about what to do with it. Moses wrote this psalm reflecting on how short a life is. Counting days sounds grim, but the verse treats it as the path to wisdom. People who know their time is limited tend to spend it on what matters, which is a good way to retire.

Common questions

What does the Bible say about retirement?

It doesn't mention retirement directly. It does speak about aging, fruitfulness in later life (Psalm 92:14), and passing on what you've learned (Psalm 71:18).

Is there a Bible verse for someone retiring?

2 Timothy 4:7 is the classic for finishing well. For a card, see the retirement card page, which has shorter options.
