Bible verses before a job interview

An interview is an hour where you're judged, which is why even confident people get nervous. These verses are about steadiness, speaking well, and holding the outcome loosely. None of them promise you'll get the job.

A verse to start with

For a job interview

Exodus 4:12

Now therefore go, and I will be with your mouth, and teach you what you shall speak.

World English Bible (WEB)

Why this fits

Moses told God he wasn't a good speaker. God's answer: 'Go, and I will be with your mouth, and teach you what you shall speak.' The verse doesn't promise eloquence; it promises company in the speaking. For the moment before an interview, that's the right promise.

7 verses for a job interview

  1. Exodus 4:12

    Now therefore go, and I will be with your mouth, and teach you what you shall speak.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Narrative

    Moses told God he wasn't a good speaker. God's answer: 'Go, and I will be with your mouth, and teach you what you shall speak.' The verse doesn't promise eloquence; it promises company in the speaking. For the moment before an interview, that's the right promise.

  2. Philippians 4:6-7

    In nothing be anxious, but in everything, by prayer and petition with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God. And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your thoughts in Christ Jesus.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Letter (epistle)

    Interview nerves are anxiety with a start time. Paul's instruction fits: name the specific worry, ask, give thanks, and let peace stand guard over your thoughts. This is the verse to read in the parking lot. 'With thanksgiving' is the part people skip. Being grateful you got the interview at all shrinks the fear of the interview. The peace Paul describes doesn't remove nerves; it keeps them from running the room.

  3. Proverbs 16:3

    Commit your deeds to Yahweh, and your plans shall succeed.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Wisdom literature

    'Commit your deeds to Yahweh, and your plans shall succeed.' The word for 'commit' means roll onto, as in rolling a weight off yourself. It's not a guarantee this specific job is yours. It's a way to walk in without carrying the whole outcome.

  4. Joshua 1:9

    Haven’t I commanded you? Be strong and courageous. Don’t be afraid. Don’t be dismayed, for Yahweh your God is with you wherever you go.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Narrative

    'Be strong and courageous' was said to a man about to take on something far bigger than a job. The reason attached, 'God is with you wherever you go,' includes conference rooms. Courage in this verse is derived from presence, not personality.

  5. Colossians 3:23-24

    And whatever you do, work heartily, as for the Lord, and not for men, knowing that from the Lord you will receive the reward of the inheritance; for you serve the Lord Christ.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Letter (epistle)

    Work 'as for the Lord.' It helps to remember who you're ultimately working for before you sit across from someone deciding whether to hire you. It lowers the stakes of the room without lowering your effort. Paul wrote this to people at the very bottom of the labor market, who had no control over their employers. If they could work with that kind of freedom, an interview candidate can walk in with some of it too.

  6. Psalm 90:17

    Let the favor of the Lord our God be on us. Establish the work of our hands for us. Yes, establish the work of our hands.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Psalm (poetry)

    'Establish the work of our hands.' A short prayer for the morning of: let what I've prepared hold up. Moses prayed it after admitting how brief life is, which is a decent antidote to treating one interview as everything. The psalm is attributed to Moses and is mostly about how short life is. Ending it with a request that our work would last is a quiet kind of confidence: not that this interview will go perfectly, but that honest work isn't wasted.

  7. James 1:5

    But if any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask of God, who gives to all liberally and without reproach, and it will be given to him.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Letter (epistle)

    You'll be asked questions you didn't prepare for. James says wisdom is available for the asking, given generously and without reproach. It's a good verse to have in mind when you need to think on your feet. The wisdom James means is practical, how to act well under pressure, not trivia. The verse also promises it's given 'without reproach.' You won't be made to feel foolish for asking, which is more than most interviews promise.

How to use these verses

Read Philippians 4:6-7 the night before and Exodus 4:12 the morning of. Say Psalm 90:17 once before you walk in. Then do the interview. If you don't get the job, come back to Proverbs 16:3; it was never a guarantee, and it still holds.

Common questions

What is a good Bible verse to read before an interview?

Exodus 4:12 is specific to speaking under pressure. Philippians 4:6-7 is for the nerves. Joshua 1:9 is for courage.

Is there a Bible verse for getting a job?

No verse promises a particular job. Proverbs 16:3 and Psalm 90:17 are prayers to commit your work and its outcome to God, which is a different and more honest thing.
