Bible verses while waiting for test results

Waiting for results is a specific kind of hard: nothing to do, no news, every phone call a possible verdict. These verses are for the in-between days. One of them is literally about not being afraid of bad news.

If the waiting is becoming unbearable, tell your care team; they can sometimes give a timeline or help you plan for either result.

A verse to start with

For waiting for test results

Psalm 112:7

He will not be afraid of evil news. His heart is steadfast, trusting in Yahweh.

World English Bible (WEB)

Why this fits

'He will not be afraid of evil news. His heart is steadfast, trusting in Yahweh.' A verse about bad news, written before the news arrives. The steadiness isn't from knowing the result; it's from trusting regardless. Few verses fit this exact situation as well.

7 verses for waiting for test results

  1. Psalm 112:7

    He will not be afraid of evil news. His heart is steadfast, trusting in Yahweh.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Psalm (poetry)

    'He will not be afraid of evil news. His heart is steadfast, trusting in Yahweh.' A verse about bad news, written before the news arrives. The steadiness isn't from knowing the result; it's from trusting regardless. Few verses fit this exact situation as well.

  2. Psalm 56:3

    When I am afraid, I will put my trust in you.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Psalm (poetry)

    'When I am afraid, I will put my trust in you.' Not if; when. David wrote it as a captive. The verse assumes fear will come in waves and gives one thing to do each time it does. The next verse adds 'I will not be afraid,' which sounds like a contradiction until you see it as a sequence. Fear arrives, trust is chosen, fear loses its grip. Then it arrives again. David's psalm is honest that this is a cycle, not a cure.

  3. Philippians 4:6-7

    In nothing be anxious, but in everything, by prayer and petition with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God. And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your thoughts in Christ Jesus.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Letter (epistle)

    Every possible result generates its own worry. Paul's instruction is to bring each one as a request, with thanks. The peace that follows guards your thoughts, which during a wait like this run in circles. 'In everything' is the key phrase for a wait like this, because the mind produces a new scenario every hour. Each one can be handed over in turn. Peace that 'surpasses understanding' means it can come before the results do.

  4. Psalm 46:10

    “Be still, and know that I am God. I will be exalted among the nations. I will be exalted in the earth.”

    World English Bible (WEB) · Psalm (poetry)

    'Be still, and know that I am God.' In context it's said in the middle of upheaval, with nations raging. Being still while waiting for a result isn't passivity; it's a decision to stop rehearsing outcomes. The knowing is the anchor.

  5. Isaiah 26:3

    You will keep whoever’s mind is steadfast in perfect peace, because he trusts in you.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Prophecy

    'Perfect peace' for the one whose mind is fixed. A mind waiting for results is the opposite of fixed; it jumps between scenarios. This verse names the alternative: keep bringing the mind back to the same place. Isaiah wrote this as a song for a city under threat. The word for 'stayed' means propped up or leaned. A mind can't stop imagining outcomes, but it can keep being leaned back onto something steadier than the next phone call.

  6. Psalm 27:14

    Wait for Yahweh. Be strong, and let your heart take courage. Yes, wait for Yahweh.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Psalm (poetry)

    'Wait for Yahweh. Be strong, and let your heart take courage. Yes, wait.' Waiting said twice, with courage in the middle. It's the verse for the day the results were supposed to come and didn't. The whole psalm is David steadying himself, moving between confidence and fear. It ends here, with waiting as the last word. He doesn't say the waiting will be short or that the news will be good. He says it can be done with courage.

  7. Romans 8:26

    In the same way, the Spirit also helps our weaknesses, for we don’t know how to pray as we ought. But the Spirit himself makes intercession for us with groanings which can’t be uttered.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Letter (epistle)

    'We don't know how to pray as we ought, but the Spirit himself makes intercession for us with groanings which can't be uttered.' Waiting for a diagnosis, you may not know what to ask for. This verse says you don't have to. The groaning is the prayer.

How to use these verses

Memorize Psalm 112:7. It's one sentence, and it's the one you'll want when the phone rings. If you can't pray in words, Romans 8:26 says that's fine.

Common questions

What Bible verse is good while waiting for medical results?

Psalm 112:7 is the closest match in Scripture: "He will not be afraid of evil news." Psalm 56:3 is the shortest.

What does the Bible say about fear of bad news?

Psalm 112:7 says the person who trusts God has a steadfast heart and doesn't fear bad news. The steadiness comes from trust, not from a promise that the news will be good.
