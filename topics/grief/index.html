Bible verses for grief

Grief doesn’t follow a schedule, and the Bible doesn’t pretend it does. The verses here come from people who lost children, friends, homes, and whole cities, and wrote it down. Most of them don’t explain the loss. They stay in it.

If your grief has become a weight you can’t carry alone, or you’ve had thoughts of not wanting to be here, please reach out to someone today: a friend, a doctor, or a crisis line in your country. That isn’t a failure of faith.

A verse to start with

For grief

Psalm 34:18

Yahweh is near to those who have a broken heart, and saves those who have a crushed spirit.

World English Bible (WEB)

Why this fits

David wrote this psalm after a humiliating escape, pretending to be insane to get out of an enemy court. It isn’t a triumphant psalm; it’s the writing of someone who has been reduced. ‘Near to those who have a broken heart’ doesn’t promise the heart will be fixed on a timetable. It says the nearness is a response to the breaking, not to recovery from it. That’s why this is the verse to start with: it meets you where grief actually puts you, on the floor, and says that is exactly where God comes close.

9 verses for grief

  1. Psalm 34:18

    Yahweh is near to those who have a broken heart, and saves those who have a crushed spirit.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Psalm (poetry)

    David wrote this psalm after a humiliating escape, pretending to be insane to get out of an enemy court. It isn’t a triumphant psalm; it’s the writing of someone who has been reduced. ‘Near to those who have a broken heart’ doesn’t promise the heart will be fixed on a timetable. It says the nearness is a response to the breaking, not to recovery from it. That’s why this is the verse to start with: it meets you where grief actually puts you, on the floor, and says that is exactly where God comes close.

  2. John 11:35

    Jesus wept.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Gospel (Jesus’ words and life)

    The shortest verse in the Bible, and it happens minutes before Jesus raises Lazarus. He knows what he’s about to do, and he weeps anyway, because Mary is weeping and the loss is real. The verse gives you permission that no explanation could: if Jesus grieved at a tomb he was about to empty, grief is not a lack of faith. It’s the correct response to loss. You don’t have to reason your way out of it before you’re allowed to feel it.

  3. Matthew 5:4

    Blessed are those who mourn, for they shall be comforted.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Gospel (Jesus’ words and life)

    The second Beatitude, and an odd one. Jesus calls mourners blessed, not because grief is pleasant but because of what is promised to them: comfort. Notice he doesn’t say when. The sentence holds the sorrow and the comfort together without collapsing one into the other. Read it slowly on a day when the loss is loud. It doesn’t tell you to stop mourning; it tells you what mourning is headed toward.

  4. Psalm 30:5

    For his anger is but for a moment. His favor is for a lifetime. Weeping may stay for the night, but joy comes in the morning.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Psalm (poetry)

    David wrote this looking back on a season he thought he wouldn’t survive. ‘Weeping may stay for the night’ is honest that the night is real and can be long; the Hebrew pictures weeping as a guest who lodges overnight. The joy that comes in the morning isn’t a promise that morning comes tomorrow. It’s the testimony of someone who has been through the night and is telling you it does end. That’s a different kind of comfort than being told to cheer up.

  5. 2 Corinthians 1:3-4

    Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies and God of all comfort; who comforts us in all our affliction, that we may be able to comfort those who are in any affliction, through the comfort with which we ourselves are comforted by God.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Letter (epistle)

    Paul opens this letter after a stretch he describes a few verses later as so hard he despaired of life itself. So ‘the God of all comfort’ isn’t a greeting-card phrase; it comes from a man who needed comforting and got it. The verse also says what comfort is for: so that we can comfort others. Grief connects you to everyone who has grieved. Eventually, though not yet, yours may be what someone else needs.

  6. Psalm 56:8

    You count my wanderings. You put my tears into your container. Aren’t they in your book?

    World English Bible (WEB) · Psalm (poetry)

    ‘You put my tears into your container’ is a strange, tender image. In the ancient world, mourners sometimes kept tears in small bottles. The psalmist imagines God doing that: collecting each one, writing it down, missing none. On the days grief feels invisible to everyone around you, this verse says it is fully seen. Not fixed, not explained. Counted.

  7. John 11:25-26

    Jesus said to her, “I am the resurrection and the life. He who believes in me will still live, even if he dies. Whoever lives and believes in me will never die. Do you believe this?”

    World English Bible (WEB) · Words of Jesus in red · Gospel (Jesus’ words and life)

    Spoken to Martha at her brother’s tomb, before anything has changed. Jesus doesn’t say ‘I will do a resurrection’; he says ‘I am the resurrection,’ and then asks her, with the loss still fresh, whether she believes it. It’s the most direct thing the Bible says about death, and it’s said to a grieving woman, not a classroom. If you can’t answer Martha’s question yet, that’s allowed. She said yes with tears on her face.

  8. 1 Thessalonians 4:13

    But we don’t want you to be ignorant, brothers, concerning those who have fallen asleep, so that you don’t grieve like the rest, who have no hope.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Letter (epistle)

    Paul is writing to a young church whose members have started dying, and the survivors don’t know what to think. His line is careful: not ‘don’t grieve’ but ‘don’t grieve like those who have no hope.’ Grief is assumed. Hope changes its shape, not its existence. This is the verse for a Christian who feels guilty for being devastated. Paul expected you to be.

  9. Revelation 21:4

    He will wipe away every tear from their eyes. Death will be no more; neither will there be mourning, nor crying, nor pain, any more. The first things have passed away.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Apocalyptic vision

    The end of the story. John’s vision of the new creation ends with a hand wiping away tears, and a list of things that will be gone: death, mourning, crying, pain. The verse only means something because those things are real now. It’s a promise for later, and it’s honest about that. On the worst days, some people find it helps to know the last page has already been written.

How to use these verses

Don’t read all of these at once. Pick one and keep it for a week. Grief is a long road, and one verse carried well does more than nine read quickly. If you’re looking for something to write for someone else, the sympathy card page has shorter verses and suggested wording.

Common questions

Is it wrong to be angry at God while grieving?

The psalms are full of people angry at God, and they were kept in the Bible on purpose. Psalm 13 opens with ‘How long, Yahweh? Will you forget me forever?’ Honest anger addressed to God is prayer. It’s silence that the psalms seem to worry about.

How long is grief supposed to last?

The Bible gives no timeline. Jacob mourned Joseph for years. Jesus wept at a tomb he was about to empty. Anyone who tells you grief should be over by a certain date is speaking for themselves, not for Scripture.

What’s the best Bible verse for grief?

For most people, Psalm 34:18, because it doesn’t require you to feel anything but broken. If you want one from Jesus, Matthew 5:4. If you want one that names the future, Revelation 21:4.
