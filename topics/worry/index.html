Bible verses for worry

Worry has a way of borrowing trouble from next week and spending it today. These passages don't pretend problems aren't real. They're mostly about where you put the weight.

A verse to start with

For worry

Matthew 6:34

Therefore don’t be anxious for tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself. Each day’s own evil is sufficient.

World English Bible (WEB)

Why this fits

Jesus doesn't claim tomorrow will be easy. The point is narrower: tomorrow will bring its own concerns, and carrying them today only doubles the load. This is the last line of a longer section in Matthew 6 about birds, flowers, and a Father who already knows what you need. Read the whole stretch and the theme is trust, not denial.

7 verses for worry

  1. Matthew 6:34

    Therefore don’t be anxious for tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself. Each day’s own evil is sufficient.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Gospel (Jesus’ words and life)

    Jesus doesn't claim tomorrow will be easy. The point is narrower: tomorrow will bring its own concerns, and carrying them today only doubles the load. This is the last line of a longer section in Matthew 6 about birds, flowers, and a Father who already knows what you need. Read the whole stretch and the theme is trust, not denial.

  2. Philippians 4:6-7

    In nothing be anxious, but in everything, by prayer and petition with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God. And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your thoughts in Christ Jesus.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Letter (epistle)

    Paul wrote this from custody, to a church he loved, so it isn't advice from a comfortable distance. The instruction is concrete: turn each worry into a specific request, and add thanks. The promise that follows isn't that the problem disappears but that peace 'guards' the heart, a military word for a sentry at a gate.

  3. 1 Peter 5:7

    casting all your worries on him, because he cares for you.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Letter (epistle)

    One short sentence with a reason attached: because he cares for you. Peter is writing to scattered, pressured Christians, and the verse before it is about humility. Worry often hides a quiet assumption that everything depends on us. This line pushes back on that assumption gently.

  4. Matthew 6:26

    See the birds of the sky, that they don’t sow, neither do they reap, nor gather into barns. Your heavenly Father feeds them. Aren’t you of much more value than they?

    World English Bible (WEB) · Gospel (Jesus’ words and life)

    Birds don't plan, and they're fed anyway. Jesus isn't telling anyone to stop working; he's asking a question about worth. If God provides for creatures that can't plan at all, the anxious math about your own future is missing a variable.

  5. Psalm 55:22

    Cast your burden on Yahweh and he will sustain you. He will never allow the righteous to be moved.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Psalm (poetry)

    David wrote this psalm while being betrayed by a close friend, which is worth knowing. 'Cast your burden' is not a tidy religious phrase here; it's what you say when the situation is real and you're out of options. The psalm promises sustaining, not that the betrayal reverses.

  6. Isaiah 26:3

    You will keep whoever’s mind is steadfast in perfect peace, because he trusts in you.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Prophecy

    The peace described here is tied to a fixed mind: attention that keeps returning to God rather than circling the problem. It's part of a song about a secure city. Worry is often exactly the opposite, a mind that won't stay anywhere. The verse names the alternative without shaming the struggle.

  7. Psalm 94:19

    In the multitude of my thoughts within me, your comforts delight my soul.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Psalm (poetry)

    This is one of the more honest lines in the Psalms about an overactive mind. The writer doesn't claim the thoughts stop. He says that in the middle of them, comfort arrives. If your worry shows up as a crowd of competing thoughts, this verse describes your situation accurately.

How to use these verses

Pick one, not seven. Read the surrounding paragraph so you know who's speaking and to whom. If a verse names a specific worry you carry, try saying that worry out loud as a request, which is what Philippians 4:6 actually describes.

Common questions

What is the most well-known Bible verse about worry?

Matthew 6:34 ("don't be anxious for tomorrow") and Philippians 4:6-7 are the two people quote most. Both come from longer passages that are worth reading in full.

Does the Bible say worrying is a sin?

The passages above treat worry as something to be redirected rather than a charge to feel guilty about. Jesus and Paul both give instructions (bring it as a request, consider the birds) rather than condemnation. Persistent, overwhelming anxiety can also be a health matter, and talking to a doctor is a reasonable step alongside prayer.
