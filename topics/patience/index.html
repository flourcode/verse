Bible verses for patience

The Bible uses two kinds of patience: putting up with people, and waiting out a season. Both are hard for different reasons. These verses cover both, and none of them pretend it comes naturally.

A verse to start with

For patience

James 5:7-8

Be patient therefore, brothers, until the coming of the Lord. Behold, the farmer waits for the precious fruit of the earth, being patient over it, until it receives the early and late rain. You also be patient. Establish your hearts, for the coming of the Lord is at hand.

World English Bible (WEB)

Why this fits

James uses a farmer who's planted and now can't do anything but wait for rain. The waiting isn't passive; he's 'patient over it,' attentive. 'Establish your hearts' means settle them, stop them swinging. For a season where you've done what you can and now have to wait, this is the picture.

7 verses for patience

  1. James 5:7-8

    Be patient therefore, brothers, until the coming of the Lord. Behold, the farmer waits for the precious fruit of the earth, being patient over it, until it receives the early and late rain. You also be patient. Establish your hearts, for the coming of the Lord is at hand.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Letter (epistle)

    James uses a farmer who's planted and now can't do anything but wait for rain. The waiting isn't passive; he's 'patient over it,' attentive. 'Establish your hearts' means settle them, stop them swinging. For a season where you've done what you can and now have to wait, this is the picture.

  2. Galatians 5:22-23

    But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faith, gentleness, and self-control. Against such things there is no law.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Letter (epistle)

    Patience is listed among the fruit of the Spirit, which matters: fruit grows, it isn't manufactured. Paul places it between peace and kindness. If you're trying to force patience by gritting your teeth, this verse suggests a different approach, one that treats it as something produced in you over time.

  3. Proverbs 14:29

    He who is slow to anger has great understanding, but he who has a quick temper displays folly.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Wisdom literature

    A proverb contrasts two people: the slow-to-anger person has great understanding, and the quick-tempered one puts foolishness on display. The point isn't that anger is always wrong but that speed is the problem. Patience here is mostly about the gap between feeling and reacting.

  4. Proverbs 16:32

    One who is slow to anger is better than the mighty; one who rules his spirit, than he who takes a city.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Wisdom literature

    A person who rules their own temper is compared favorably to a general capturing a city. In the ancient world that's about the highest praise available. If patience feels unimpressive next to action, this proverb ranks it higher. The pairing is deliberate: 'slow to anger' and 'rules his spirit' are one skill, seen from the inside and the outside. It takes years to build, like a city takes years to take. Proverbs treats it as the more lasting conquest.

  5. Ephesians 4:2-3

    with all lowliness and humility, with patience, bearing with one another in love, being eager to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Letter (epistle)

    'Bearing with one another in love' is patience applied to specific people you're stuck with. Paul writes it to a church trying to stay unified across real differences. The verse doesn't say pretend the friction isn't there; it says carry it, for the sake of keeping peace.

  6. Ecclesiastes 7:8

    Better is the end of a thing than its beginning. The patient in spirit is better than the proud in spirit.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Wisdom literature

    'The patient in spirit is better than the proud in spirit' pairs patience with humility and impatience with pride, which is often accurate. And 'the end of a thing is better than its beginning' is a reminder that how something finishes matters more than how it started.

  7. Romans 8:25

    But if we hope for that which we don’t see, we wait for it with patience.

    World English Bible (WEB) · Letter (epistle)

    Paul defines patience as waiting for what you don't see yet. That's the whole difficulty. If you could see it, you wouldn't need patience. The verse comes in a passage about creation itself groaning and waiting, which puts your own waiting in a very large context.

Common questions

What Bible verse says patience is a virtue?

That exact phrase isn't in the Bible. The closest ideas are Galatians 5:22 (patience as fruit of the Spirit) and Proverbs 16:32 (ruling your temper is better than taking a city).

Is there a Bible verse about being patient with people?

Ephesians 4:2 ("bearing with one another in love") and Proverbs 14:29 both deal with patience toward other people specifically.
