This week’s verse

Psalm 46:1

“God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble.”

World English Bible (WEB)

The psalm goes on to describe the earth giving way and mountains sliding into the sea, then nations in uproar, then a command: be still. It was written for a city under threat, and it doesn’t pretend the threat away. What it insists on is that the refuge is “very present,” meaning here, now, available before the shaking stops. You don’t have to reach a calm place first.

A question to carry: Where do you go for refuge when things shake, and is it holding?

For someone you care about

Bible verses for work stress. Work stress is usually a mix of too much to do and too much riding on it. The Bible takes work seriously, more seriously than most religions, and it also insists on rest. These verses hold both.

Matthew 11:28-29

“Come to me, all you who labor and are heavily burdened, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you, and learn from me, for I am gentle and humble in heart; and you will find rest for your souls.”

World English Bible (WEB)

'All you who labor and are heavily burdened' is a direct address to the overworked. Jesus doesn't offer to remove the work. He offers rest 'for your souls' and a yoke that's easy, which in farming means one that fits. Stress often means carrying a yoke that doesn't.

If you’re writing it in a card or a text: “Thinking of you today. This came to mind and I wanted you to have it.”

For those who comfort others

Fear and anxiety. Give them a practice, not a feeling: each worry as a request, with thanks. Avoid ‘God won’t give you more than you can handle,’ which isn’t in the Bible.

Philippians 4:6-7

“In nothing be anxious, but in everything, by prayer and petition with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God. And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your thoughts in Christ Jesus.”

World English Bible (WEB)

Is that in the Bible?

“Cleanliness is next to godliness.” Not in the Bible. It comes from a John Wesley sermon in the 1700s, and possibly older Hebrew proverbs. The Bible does talk about clean hands and a pure heart (Psalm 24:4), which is a different idea.
