A verse for a job interview.

Once a week: a verse read slowly, something for someone you care about, something for those who comfort others, and a little help separating what the Bible says from what we think it says. Glad you're here.

THIS WEEK'S VERSE
Proverbs 3:5-6
"Trust in Yahweh with all your heart, and don’t lean on your own understanding. In all your ways acknowledge him, and he will make your paths straight."
(World English Bible)

This isn’t a warning against thinking. Proverbs is a book that prizes
thinking. The warning is against leaning your whole weight on your own
understanding, as if it were the final word. Trust here is a posture
toward God taken with your whole heart, and the promise that follows is
about direction, not outcome: paths made straight, not paths made easy.

A question to carry: Is there a decision you've been making alone that you could bring to God and to a trusted person?

FOR SOMEONE YOU CARE ABOUT
Bible verses before a job interview. An interview is an hour where you're judged, which is why even confident
people get nervous. These verses are about steadiness, speaking well,
and holding the outcome loosely. None of them promise you'll get the
job.

Exodus 4:12
"Now therefore go, and I will be with your mouth, and teach you what you shall speak."

Moses told God he wasn't a good speaker. God's answer: 'Go, and I will
be with your mouth, and teach you what you shall speak.' The verse
doesn't promise eloquence; it promises company in the speaking. For the
moment before an interview, that's the right promise.

If you're writing it in a card or a text: "Thinking of you today. This came to mind and I wanted you to have it."

IS THAT IN THE BIBLE?
"this too shall pass" — Not a Bible verse; it's a Persian proverb. The nearest biblical idea is
2 Corinthians 4:17, that present trouble is light and momentary compared
with what's coming, and Psalm 30:5 about weeping lasting the night but
joy coming in the morning.
