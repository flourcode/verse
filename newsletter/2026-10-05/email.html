This week’s verse

Proverbs 3:5-6

“Trust in Yahweh with all your heart, and don’t lean on your own understanding. In all your ways acknowledge him, and he will make your paths straight.”

World English Bible (WEB)

This isn’t a warning against thinking. Proverbs is a book that prizes thinking. The warning is against leaning your whole weight on your own understanding, as if it were the final word. Trust here is a posture toward God taken with your whole heart, and the promise that follows is about direction, not outcome: paths made straight, not paths made easy.

A question to carry: Is there a decision you've been making alone that you could bring to God and to a trusted person?

For someone you care about

Bible verses before a job interview. An interview is an hour where you're judged, which is why even confident people get nervous. These verses are about steadiness, speaking well, and holding the outcome loosely. None of them promise you'll get the job.

Exodus 4:12

“Now therefore go, and I will be with your mouth, and teach you what you shall speak.”

World English Bible (WEB)

Moses told God he wasn't a good speaker. God's answer: 'Go, and I will be with your mouth, and teach you what you shall speak.' The verse doesn't promise eloquence; it promises company in the speaking. For the moment before an interview, that's the right promise.

If you’re writing it in a card or a text: “Thinking of you today. This came to mind and I wanted you to have it.”

Is that in the Bible?

“This too shall pass.” Not a Bible verse; it's a Persian proverb. The nearest biblical idea is 2 Corinthians 4:17, that present trouble is light and momentary compared with what's coming, and Psalm 30:5 about weeping lasting the night but joy coming in the morning.

2 Corinthians 4:17

“For our light affliction, which is for the moment, works for us more and more exceedingly an eternal weight of glory,”
