A verse for after losing a job.

Once a week: a verse read slowly, something for someone you care about, something for those who comfort others, and a little help separating what the Bible says from what we think it says. Glad you're here.

THIS WEEK'S VERSE
Isaiah 41:10
"Don’t you be afraid, for I am with you. Don’t be dismayed, for I am your God. I will strengthen you. Yes, I will help you. Yes, I will uphold you with the right hand of my righteousness."
(World English Bible)

Spoken to exiles in Babylon who had every reason to be afraid: their
city was gone and their captors were the most powerful empire on earth.
God gives them five short promises in one breath. The last is the most
physical: a hand holding them up. Whatever else is uncertain, the verse
says, the grip isn’t.

A question to carry: Which of the five promises in this verse do you need most today?

FOR SOMEONE YOU CARE ABOUT
Bible verses after losing a job. Losing a job is a financial problem and an identity problem at the same
time, and the second one often hurts more. These verses speak to both.
They don't promise the next job by Friday. They do say you're not
forgotten.

Psalm 34:18
"Yahweh is near to those who have a broken heart, and saves those who have a crushed spirit."

'Yahweh is near to those who have a broken heart.' Job loss can break
something, especially when it wasn't your choice. This verse doesn't
offer a plan; it offers nearness, and it's addressed specifically to the
crushed. That's the first thing to hear, before any verse about
provision.

If you're writing it in a card or a text: "Thinking of you today. This came to mind and I wanted you to have it."

FOR THOSE WHO COMFORT OTHERS
Work and money. For job loss: the birds are fed. Avoid Jeremiah 29:11 as a promise of a
quick new job; it was written to people staying in exile seventy years.

Matthew 6:26
"See the birds of the sky, that they don’t sow, neither do they reap, nor gather into barns. Your heavenly Father feeds them. Aren’t you of much more value than they?"

IS THAT IN THE BIBLE?
"when god closes a door he opens a window" — Not in the Bible (it's usually traced to a 1600s Irish proverb, and The
Sound of Music). The closest verse is Revelation 3:8, where Jesus tells
a small church he has set before them an open door that no one can shut.

Revelation 3:8
"“I know your works (behold, I have set before you an open door, which no one can shut), that you have a little power, and kept my word, and didn’t deny my name."
