This week’s verse

Matthew 6:34

“Therefore don’t be anxious for tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself. Each day’s own evil is sufficient.”

World English Bible (WEB)

This is the last line of a longer passage in Matthew 6 where Jesus points at birds and wildflowers and asks why people who are worth more than either spend so much energy on worry. He doesn’t claim tomorrow will be easy; he says it will have its own trouble. The point is narrower and more useful than “don’t worry”: today’s burden is enough, and borrowing tomorrow’s on top of it only doubles the weight.

A question to carry: What are you carrying today that actually belongs to a later day?

For someone you care about

Bible verses for starting over. Starting over means something ended, and usually not the way you'd have chosen. The Bible is full of people who had to begin again: Abraham leaving home, Israel leaving Egypt, Paul leaving his old life. These verses are about the next step.

Isaiah 43:18-19

“Don’t remember the former things, and don’t consider the things of old. Behold, I will do a new thing. It springs out now. Don’t you know it? I will even make a way in the wilderness, and rivers in the desert.”

World English Bible (WEB)

'Don't remember the former things' and 'I will do a new thing' were spoken to exiles who couldn't stop looking back at what they'd lost. God doesn't erase the past; he announces something new and asks, 'Don't you know it?' as if it's already sprouting. A way in the wilderness is exactly what starting over feels like.

If you’re writing it in a card or a text: “Thinking of you today. This came to mind and I wanted you to have it.”

Is that in the Bible?

“God helps those who help themselves.” Not in the Bible. It's from Benjamin Franklin's Poor Richard's Almanack (and older Greek sources). The Bible tends to say the opposite: God helps those who can't help themselves (Psalm 34:18, Romans 5:6).
