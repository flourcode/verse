A verse for starting over.

Once a week I send one verse read slowly, one situation someone near you is probably in, and one note for those whose job is to comfort others. Glad you're here.

THIS WEEK'S VERSE
Matthew 6:34
"Therefore don’t be anxious for tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself. Each day’s own evil is sufficient."
(World English Bible)

This is the last line of a longer passage in Matthew 6 where Jesus
points at birds and wildflowers and asks why people who are worth more
than either spend so much energy on worry. He doesn’t claim tomorrow
will be easy; he says it will have its own trouble. The point is
narrower and more useful than “don’t worry”: today’s burden is enough,
and borrowing tomorrow’s on top of it only doubles the weight.

A question to carry: What are you carrying today that actually belongs to a later day?

FOR SOMEONE YOU CARE ABOUT
Bible verses for starting over. Starting over means something ended, and usually not the way you'd have
chosen. The Bible is full of people who had to begin again: Abraham
leaving home, Israel leaving Egypt, Paul leaving his old life. These
verses are about the next step.

Isaiah 43:18-19
"Don’t remember the former things, and don’t consider the things of old. Behold, I will do a new thing. It springs out now. Don’t you know it? I will even make a way in the wilderness, and rivers in the desert."

'Don't remember the former things' and 'I will do a new thing' were
spoken to exiles who couldn't stop looking back at what they'd lost. God
doesn't erase the past; he announces something new and asks, 'Don't you
know it?' as if it's already sprouting. A way in the wilderness is
exactly what starting over feels like.

If you're writing it in a card or a text: "Thinking of you today. This came to mind and I wanted you to have it."

FOR THOSE WHO COMFORT OTHERS
Fear and anxiety. For someone about to walk into something. Courage as a conclusion from
who is with them.

Joshua 1:9
"Haven’t I commanded you? Be strong and courageous. Don’t be afraid. Don’t be dismayed, for Yahweh your God is with you wherever you go.”"

The full quick reference: https://betterverses.com/ministry/pastoral-care-scripture/

IS THAT IN THE BIBLE?
"god helps those who help themselves" — Not in the Bible. It's from Benjamin Franklin's Poor Richard's Almanack
(and older Greek sources). The Bible tends to say the opposite: God
helps those who can't help themselves (Psalm 34:18, Romans 5:6).

READ IT IN CONTEXT
Philippians 4:13: Paul wrote it from a prison, about having nothing. https://betterverses.com/verses/philippians-4-13/
